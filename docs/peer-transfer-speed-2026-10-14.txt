Connected-peer transfer audit — October 14, 2026

Scope and method
One real Samsung phone (R3GYC0H7PMY, com.axonic.dev) and existing AVD
Medium_Phone_API_36.1 (emulator-5554, com.axonic development client).
The emulator client needed a data-preserving native rebuild after it failed
with missing ExpoSecureStore. After inspection/startup its account was empty;
a synthetic test identity was created, with credentials in the existing
protected test helper. No existing identity was removed or overwritten.
The official phone app was not installed or changed. No production build.

Synthetic files contain byte i modulo 251. Sizes are 524287, 2097151 and
5242879 bytes. They use queueRootAttachmentSource and normal chat delivery,
not an isolated socket benchmark. Temporary wrappers observed enqueue,
completion and direct-stream lifecycle; they were restored afterwards.
Temporary Dev diagnostics were removed after observation. No transfer
algorithm or protocol fix is claimed in this audit.

Times are measured within each sender process; emulator and phone clocks
differ, so timestamps across devices must not be subtracted. Total time runs
from queue invocation to signed attachment completion. Preparation is the
runtime's stage counter; the wider enqueue/preparation interval includes
queueing and bookkeeping. Stream time includes lane setup/handshake and
receiver hash verification. Stage totals may overlap and are not additive.

All six listed files were delivered, receiver SHA-256 matched the original,
and their attachment jobs cleared. A further intermediate 2 MiB transfer
also completed, but its timing observers were lost on a development refresh;
it is excluded from the timing table, not treated as a fast/successful timing.

Findings
1. Connection bandwidth is not the sole bottleneck. The verified direct stream
   moved 2 MiB in under one second and 5 MiB in about 1.7 seconds. The latter
   is roughly 2.9 MiB/s including stream setup and final verification.
2. Preparation dominates the stable 5 MiB run: 5.777 seconds of encryption,
   hashing/tree work and scheduling; queue-to-enqueued descriptor was 6.099s.
   Merely increasing network chunks would not remove this cost.
3. Earlier runs showed connected RTC peers but no direct stream, incoming
   retries before download, and hundreds of chunk requests/custody uploads.
   The warm run used the same source algorithm and succeeded through the
   stream. Instrumentation/refresh changed session conditions; this is not
   evidence that the problem was fixed by a code change.
4. The exact trigger for the initial missed stream remains unproven. Code
   inspection identifies a 60-second retry suppression after any attempted
   stream (including unavailable attempts), and 10-second source backoff plus
   exponential job retries after transfer failures. These may amplify route
   startup failures and warrant targeted tests, not blind limit increases.
5. The binary stream is restricted to the authenticated intended RTC endpoint
   over DTLS; a generic connected peer/AoN or unencrypted LAN socket is not an
   equivalent stream. A peer shown as connected does not alone prove the media
   lane is usable. This audit does not establish physical Wi-Fi-only throughput
   or whether the RTC candidate pair used TURN; no candidate statistics were
   captured. It is a small development-mode sample, not production percentiles.

Recommended next work
First reproduce stream readiness/fallback transitions deterministically and
separate unavailable-before-start from an actual failed stream, preserving
bounded retries and verified chunk fallback. Then profile/offload preparation
without changing cryptographic parameters or skipping integrity checks.
The file does not need larger network frames to explain the measured delay.
