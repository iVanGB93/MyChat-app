Group recovery protocol and rollout

Group metadata is retained by group members, not a global public address book.
On connection, the group recovery pump queues metadata for a known member.
It retries failed persistence, limits reconnect churn to once per minute per
peer, and resets its session bookkeeping when the local identity changes.

New or changed creator-owned groups get an Ed25519 root signature with domain
axonic-group-backup-v1. The canonical payload includes group ID, creator,
name, revision, ordered membership, and photo reference (absent means null).
The root public key must hash to the creator's Axonic account ID. Root private
keys never leave the account controller. Mobile-derived temporary roots are
cleared after signing. The AoN uses its existing root-key lifetime.

Members retain the signed snapshot and return it over authenticated encrypted
chat/custody transport. Only members listed in that snapshot can forward it,
and the recovering owner must also be listed. Existing higher revisions and
local block/mute choices are preserved. An empty creator ledger can restore
its own signed group automatically. These are durable public-authority proofs,
not expiring identity admission records; device-key renewal does not invalidate
them. This is not consensus: an empty device cannot prove that the highest
snapshot returned by reachable members is globally the newest revision.

Legacy unsigned metadata cannot prove creator authority after creator recovery.
It creates a recoveryPending offer, not an active group. The app displays a
Review action and asks the creator to confirm the name and full member list.
Until confirmed, it cannot send/receive group traffic, change membership,
sign backups, or answer another member's recovery request. Confirmation signs
and redistributes a backup; subsequent recovery is automatic.

Missing membership withholds message acknowledgement. Authenticated application
rejection no longer tears down a normal chat axon. Invalid ciphertext and
transport authentication failures still close the session.

Limits: recovery requires a reachable member holding metadata (or its queued
encrypted delivery); it does not restore deleted history, media files, local
nicknames, or device preferences. Existing storage bounds remain enforced.

Rollout: new recovery control tags require updated recipients. Keep app and AoN
protocol sources identical. Do not deploy proactive recovery to the live AoNs
ahead of the matching app update: old Play Store clients reject unknown control
tags. No production build or official phone installation was authorized in this
task. The current group predates signed backups and needs one local review after
the updated app is installed. Both AoNs hold its legacy membership.
